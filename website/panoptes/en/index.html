Panoptes – your BYD, awake and connected

What is this? A free app that runs on the screen in your BYD.

Who is it for? For BYD drivers who want a camera guard, a trip log and charging costs.

How do I start? Download the file and put it on the car once with a PC or laptop. We show you every step.

What Panoptes does

🛡️

Guard with AI detection

While your car is parked, the cameras watch its surroundings. You decide when it records: only on impact, with AI detection everywhere, or only at your own private places such as your garage or driveway. Without a choice it only records on impact. To protect the 12 V battery it goes to sleep; the threshold follows the battery type (lead-acid, AGM or LFP).

📹

Dashcam from 0.1.27

On hard braking or a bump Panoptes saves a short clip from all cameras by itself, about 20 seconds before and after. No continuous recording. The clips stay in the car or on the USB stick and are never sent anywhere by themselves. You can watch and save clips on your phone with the phone app (beta 4). Panoptes then asks “Keep?”, otherwise the clip is deleted after 72 hours. From 0.1.36 every clip remembers what triggered it (without location). In Switzerland the dashcam is off by default. Whether recording while driving is allowed where you live differs by country, see the notes.

📒

Trips and charging

Trips, consumption and charging sessions are recorded in the car. Panoptes shows the cost per session, per month and as average cost per 100 km, from 0.1.35 including the estimated charging loss (sign “~”). If a price is missing it says “price missing” instead of inventing a number; free charging can be entered explicitly.

🏠

Wallbox beta

Set up your wallbox via evcc, Home Assistant or Tuya, and from version 0.1.12 also directly (e.g. go-e, KEBA, openWB, Easee, Zaptec): see the status, set mode, charge limit and current. Control only while the car is parked, with confirmation and enabled per charge point. So far only checked with demo and test devices.

💬

Telegram notifications

Your own bot reports guard events, charging, a weak starter battery, tyre pressure and updates. Each category can be switched on its own, with quiet hours for the night. From 0.1.38.2: write anything to the bot and you get a menu with big buttons. From 0.1.38.11 guard pictures and clips come along by themselves (turn off with /bilder aus). How to connect Telegram

🗺️

Map with chargers

You choose where the map comes from: the community server (with terrain and free charging spots where operators provide data), OpenStreetMap, or none. Until you choose, the map stays empty. Speed cameras are shown in Austria only, off by default and with a legal notice.

🏕️

Camping mode beta

Guard “occupied”, night screen, battery protection with time left. Switch on with a tap, on your phone or with /camping in Telegram. You choose the heating type on the camping page; by default only a dry run. More

🚗

On the car screen

Panoptes runs as its own full-screen app with large buttons. While driving, settings are locked; home, navigation, map and guard stay available. At start-up you see for each service whether it is ready.

🧩

Setup assistant

On first start, three short screens guide you through the essentials, with all permissions on one screen. Everything else is under “Noch einrichten” (still to set up) later. Almost nothing is preselected (only “remember habits” is set to yes, and that stays in the car); anything that sends data out stays off until you turn it on. Whatever your car cannot do is skipped with an explanation.

🔌

“Where did you charge?”

After charging, Panoptes asks for the station and price while parked, never while driving. With “remember place” it knows the price next time. With “Laut Säule” (as shown by the charger) you enter the charger's kWh and cost; your price is then used for “Cheapest nearby” (AC and DC separately). The question can be switched off.

💳

Your charge cards in the car

Charge cards and networks, minimum power, plug and arrival charge level are set in the car. The route planner uses them; they are only stored in the car.

📍

Your places

One list for everything: home, garage, work or the charging spot with its own price. For each place you decide whether it is private (guard), a charging price applies or it is a destination in the navigation. Places never leave the car.

🌍

Remote access

Reach your car from anywhere via Tailscale. Setup uses a QR code on the car screen and your own Tailscale account. For advanced users, 0.1.14 adds an independent emergency access, off by default.

🔋

“Will it make it?”

With a route from the route planner, including waypoints, Panoptes tells you on the road whether the battery will last to the next stop and suggests a charging stop if needed.

📊

Monthly report

Distance, consumption, charging and costs, standby loss and guard events of a month at a glance, also for printing. Calculated in the car.

🔁

Switching from OverDrive

If you used the OverDrive app before, you can take over trips, charging sessions and settings, with a preview and without duplicates. Panoptes never takes over consents or risky switches; you set those yourself.

🧭

Route planner link

Connect Panoptes to your account on byd.serveone.at/ev/: show a code in the car, confirm on your phone, done. Disconnect at any time.

🖥️

EV Cockpit on your PC

The EV Cockpit shows Panoptes data on your PC, including charging sessions and costs, and can change its settings. You confirm the pairing with one tap and a four-digit check number. From Cockpit 1.23 also choose the cluster display, set up the launcher and install updates, only with your permission. From 1.24 you can also plan a route in the Cockpit and send it to the car.

⬆️

Updates in the app

If you like, Panoptes looks for new versions itself. Under Settings → All settings → App updates you choose: “Only notify”, “Small updates on their own, report big ones” (default) or “Everything automatically”. Small updates (only the last number changes) arrive quietly; Panoptes announces big ones first with a summary. If you had “Only notify”, you were switched over once and told so. It never installs while driving: if you start an update yourself, it runs right away when the car is in P, even with the ignition on; on its own only once the car has been off for at least 10 minutes; if something goes wrong, the previous version comes back by itself within 30 minutes. From 0.1.38.2 also by button from Telegram, your phone or the Cockpit, only with your permission. After an update Panoptes sets itself up again and reports when everything runs. From 0.1.13 Panoptes no longer changes permanent Android settings by itself (such as “screen always on”); values set earlier can be reset. What you change yourself (own picture, turning apps off) stays until you change it back. From 0.1.36 the logs are kept through updates.

🏕️ Camping mode in detail (beta)

Since 0.1.16.1: you sleep in the car, and Panoptes looks after power, the guard and the screen. Heating is still in beta. By default only a dry run runs; you choose the heating type on the camping page.

What it does

  • Guard “occupied”: While camping runs, the guard does not scare anyone off (no screen, lights or horn) and raises no shake alarm when you move.
  • Night screen: After a time without touch that you can set, the screen turns almost black, with a big clock and a few numbers. No sounds.
  • Battery protection and time left: You set a lower limit (20 % by default). Panoptes shows how long the battery lasts for heating and stops before the limit. Panoptes never switches the car off.

Switching on

  • With a tap on the car screen, only while parked (gear P).
  • On your phone, in Panoptes’ web interface.
  • In Telegram with /camping.
  • Scheduling for a set time (start and wake-up time) works from 0.1.17 through Panoptes’ interface; operating it from the EV Cockpit follows.
  • Camping ends by itself when you drive off.

Heating: beta

Panoptes only heats once you choose a heating type on the camping page. Until then it runs a dry run: it only shows what it would do and switches nothing.

  • Car on: Panoptes switches the climate control directly.
  • Car off: through the BYD cloud, which needs a BYD account. This wakes the car for every heating run.

Limits

  • No cabin sensor: the Dolphin Surf has none. Panoptes therefore never shows an interior temperature and heats in intervals based on the outside temperature (“every x minutes for y minutes”). You can add an external sensor if you like.
  • Consumption: roughly 1.5 to 3 % battery per hour at 0 °C while heating. That is an estimate, not a measurement; Panoptes learns from your real consumption.
  • After DC charging ends: BYD then switches the car off by itself.
  • Camping is no substitute for supervision. Never leave children or animals alone in the car.

Cluster: navigation and apps on the instrument cluster

On some BYD models Panoptes shows its own display, a navigation app like Google Maps or Waze, or another app you allow on the instrument cluster.

How it works:

  1. In Panoptes: Apps & display → Display → What does the cluster show?
  2. Tap a display, a navigation app or an app.
  3. Back to the BYD cluster: Cluster back to normal.

Help us: Under Apps & display → “What can my cluster do?” you see what your cluster can do. Do you have a different model? Tell us through the wish box your model and whether it works (yes or no).

Good to know:

  • By default, while driving the cluster only shows navigation apps from a fixed list (e.g. Google Maps, Waze, OsmAnd, ABRP). Other apps only run while parked.
  • You can switch on “also while driving” for each app after a one-time notice. You are then responsible yourself. Watching videos while driving is illegal in many countries.
  • The first time, you confirm a notice: the speed and warning lights must stay visible.
  • It does not work on the Dolphin Surf and Seagull. On cars with DiLink 5 it is locked for now.

Detailed guide (8 steps)
  1. Read first. Panoptes is meant for your own car, for private use and at your own risk. Read the notes. To install it you turn on developer functions in the car. If in doubt whether this matters for your warranty, ask your dealer.
  2. Download. Download panoptes-0.1.40-f0453d5.apk to your PC or laptop with the button above. Optional, for experienced users: compare the checksum first. On Windows type certutil -hashfile panoptes-0.1.40-f0453d5.apk SHA256 in a command prompt, on a Mac or Linux shasum -a 256 panoptes-0.1.40-f0453d5.apk. The output must match the checksum above exactly. If it differs, do not install the file.
  3. Turn on developer access (ADB) in the car. Panoptes starts its background services, for example for the cameras, through the car's ADB interface. This connection stays inside the car (127.0.0.1). It is off by default. You turn it on in the BYD developer menu, with the switches for USB debugging or wireless ADB. How to reach this menu depends a lot on model and software version; on newer BYD software it is locked with a code. So far we have only tested this step on the Dolphin Surf (DiLink 3). Without it Panoptes will not fully work.
  4. Get the app onto the car. Tested way, once with a PC or laptop: install Google's Android platform tools (they contain adb). PC and car must be on the same network, for example your phone's Wi-Fi hotspot. Then type adb connect <car-address>:5555 and adb install -r panoptes-0.1.40-f0453d5.apk. You find the car's address in its Wi-Fi settings or in your hotspot's device list. At the end it must say Success. After that, updates arrive in the app by themselves. An Android phone instead of a PC (not yet tested by us): this also works with an adb app on the phone, on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot as the car, see the guide. It does not work with an iPhone. USB stick or BYD file manager: being checked. The file manager passes the file to the installer after a warning; whether it then completes is not confirmed yet. If Android asks about “installing from unknown sources”, allow it only for this installation.
  5. First start with the setup assistant. Open Panoptes on the car screen. Short steps guide you through the essentials, for example language, confirming the notes, autostart, display over other apps, permissions and USB access. Everything else (guard, map source, data sharing, speed cameras, Telegram, route planner, EV Cockpit, remote access) is under “Noch einrichten” (still to set up) afterwards. Almost nothing is preselected. Anything that sends data out stays off until you turn it on yourself.
  6. Allow autostart. For Panoptes to start by itself when you get in, it has to be allowed in BYD's app management. The setup assistant opens the right page; untick the autostart block for Panoptes there. BYD resets this switch after every installation and update. Panoptes turns it back on by itself after an update and tells you if that fails. DiLink 5 has no such app management, so the step is skipped.
  7. Confirm USB access. Android asks once “Allow USB debugging?”. Tick “Always allow from this computer” and tap Allow. The “computer” is Panoptes itself in the car. Without the tick Android asks again after every restart.
  8. Open on your phone or PC (optional). With remote access (Tailscale) set up, open your car's address in a browser. To log in you need the access code, found on the car screen under Settings → All settings → Connections → “Your access code” (from 0.1.38.14; “Show” asks first and hides it again after 60 seconds). With Telegram you can also log in with a check number. You allow other devices, such as the EV Cockpit, under Settings → All settings → Pair a device.
If something goes wrong
  • Panoptes does not start by itself after an update: allow autostart again (step 6). BYD resets it after every installation. Panoptes does this itself after an update. If a message appears, tap “Autostart wieder einschalten” (turn autostart back on) on the display.
  • Panoptes has no internet: BYD's built-in traffic monitor can block the internet for other apps. Panoptes offers to switch it off; the screen then needs a restart.
  • Cameras or guard do not run: check that USB access was confirmed with “Always allow” (step 7) and that ADB access is still on; a BYD software update can reset it.
  • Where to store recordings? Ideally on a USB stick in the car.
  • Removing it again: uninstall in the car's Android settings under Apps. Data inside the app is lost; recordings on the USB stick stay.

Privacy: what goes where

Principle: your recordings, trips and positions stay in the car. If you watch recordings on your phone on the road, they pass through our bridge end-to-end encrypted; the server cannot read them and does not store them. Most things that send data out are off by default; you turn each item on separately, and Panoptes tells you beforehand where the data goes. The exceptions are the update check and the map search, both listed in the table below. If you set up Telegram, the guard sends its pictures along (can be turned off). No ads, no tracking.

Phone and PC over the internet (bridge)

On the road, the phone app or EV Cockpit on your PC reaches your car through a small relay service on our server. It is off until you switch it on in the car. End-to-end encrypted: the server only forwards unreadable data and cannot see vehicle data, places or commands. No IP logs, only one count line per hour for the whole service. Server in the Hetzner data centre in Falkenstein, Germany. If you switch the bridge off, your mailbox is deleted. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (a feature you switch on yourself). All details

How Panoptes connects to the cockpit, route planner, map and wall box

Panoptes runs in the car. Everything else is optional and can be connected and disconnected one by one.

1Panoptes ↔ EV Cockpit (pairing)

You allow the cockpit once with a check number, in Telegram or on the Panoptes page. The cockpit then fetches vehicle data, settings and the trip log straight from the car, over your home network or Tailscale.

What stays private: remote access, password and ports. None of this goes through our servers; the trip log stays on your PC. The cockpit keeps the access key in your computer's keyring. Anything else it sends to servers (map, 3D model, route planner, usage count) needs its own tick; without a tick, nothing.

2Panoptes ↔ route planner

Once linked, your destination with charging stops goes into the car. Panoptes reports charge level, consumption factor and driving status so the plan is right. If you turn it on (off by default) and fetch your trip log on /ev/, it comes end-to-end encrypted straight from the car to your browser.

What stays private: the server cannot read the trip log; it only receives encrypted data and, according to the route planner, keeps it in memory for 2 minutes at most. Your approximate position is only sent when Panoptes suggests a new charging stop on the road.

3Panoptes ↔ community map

If you choose it as map source, map tiles and charger data come from byd.serveone.at, with live availability where open data exists. Without a choice Panoptes loads no map.

What stays private: trips and recordings. The server only sees your IP address and the map area. Shared rules only go to the community server if you share them yourself (off by default).

4Panoptes → Telegram

Your own bot sends you message texts and the guard's pictures (on by default, can be turned off). They are then stored on Telegram's servers. They often show other people.

What stays private: no videos on our servers, no detour through the project.

5Panoptes ↔ wallbox (beta)

Panoptes talks to your wallbox in your home network, via evcc, Home Assistant, Tuya or directly. Control only works while the car is parked and you have enabled the charge point, each change with confirmation. You can withdraw the permission at any time.

What stays private: none of this goes through our servers. Credentials are stored encrypted in the car. Manufacturer clouds (Easee, Zaptec, Tuya) only with your consent per device.

6Panoptes ↔ Home Assistant

Your car connects directly to your own Home Assistant, without our servers. There you see values like charge level and range, build your own automations and get notifications. This works over MQTT, which you set up in Panoptes. The dedicated integration for Home Assistant is a beta and can be installed through HACS. On the road over mobile data it only works through Tailscale.

What stays private: The data stays with you, not with us.

7EV Cockpit ↔ route planner

The cockpit shows the route waiting for you and your charge cards. When it is paired with Panoptes, only Panoptes reports the charge level, so nothing arrives twice.

What stays private: your trips stay on your PC.
