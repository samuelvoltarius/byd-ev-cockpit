Panoptes — your BYD, awake and connected

An app that runs right on your car's screen, plus an interface you open on your phone or PC: a camera guard, trips and charging with costs, your wallbox, remote access, Telegram notifications, its own map with chargers and a link to the route planner.

New, October 2026: Panoptes is now publicly available as test version 0.1.14.1, with source code and a step-by-step guide. So far it has mainly been tested on a BYD Dolphin Surf (DiLink 3). Panoptes is a private hobby project and not made by BYD. Please read the notes before installing.

What Panoptes does

🚗

On the car screen

Panoptes runs as its own full-screen app with large buttons. While driving, settings are locked; home, map and guard stay available.

🧩

Setup assistant

On first start an assistant guides you step by step through permissions, guard, map, data sharing and updates. Nothing is preselected; anything that sends data out stays off until you turn it on. Whatever your car cannot do is skipped with an explanation.

🛡️

Guard with AI detection

While your car is parked, the cameras watch its surroundings. You decide when it records: only on impact, with AI detection everywhere, or only at your own private places such as your garage or driveway. Without a choice it only records on impact. To protect the 12 V battery it goes to sleep; the threshold follows the battery type (lead-acid, AGM or LFP).

📒

Trips and charging

Trips, consumption and charging sessions are recorded in the car. Panoptes shows the cost per session, per month and as average cost per 100 km. If a price is missing it says “price missing” instead of inventing a number; free charging can be entered explicitly.

🔌

“Where did you charge?”

After charging, Panoptes asks for the station and price while parked, never while driving. With “remember place” it knows the price next time. The question can be switched off.

💳

Your charge cards in the car

Charge cards and networks, minimum power, plug and arrival charge level are set in the car. The route planner uses them; they are only stored in the car.

🏠

Wallbox in testing

Set up your wallbox via evcc, Home Assistant or Tuya, and from version 0.1.12 also directly (e.g. go-e, KEBA, openWB, Easee, Zaptec): see the status, set mode, charge limit and current. Control only while the car is parked, with confirmation and enabled per charge point. So far only checked with demo and test devices.

📍

Your places

One list for everything: home, garage, work or the charging spot with its own price. For each place you decide whether it is private (guard), a charging price applies or it is a destination in the navigation. Places never leave the car.

🌍

Remote access

Reach your car from anywhere via Tailscale. Setup uses a QR code on the car screen and your own Tailscale account. For advanced users, 0.1.14 adds an independent emergency access, off by default.

💬

Telegram notifications

Your own bot reports guard events, charging, a weak starter battery, tyre pressure and updates. Each category can be switched on its own, with quiet hours for the night. Pictures and videos only if you turn that on.

🗺️

Map with chargers

You choose where the map comes from: the community server (with terrain and free charging spots where operators provide data), OpenStreetMap, or none. Until you choose, the map stays empty. Speed cameras are shown in Austria only, off by default and with a legal notice.

🔋

“Will it make it?”

With a route from the route planner, including waypoints, Panoptes tells you on the road whether the battery will last to the next stop and suggests a charging stop if needed.

📊

Monthly report

Distance, consumption, charging and costs, standby loss and guard events of a month at a glance, also for printing. Calculated in the car.

🔁

Switching from OverDrive

If you used the OverDrive app before, you can take over trips, charging sessions and settings, with a preview and without duplicates. Panoptes never takes over consents or risky switches; you set those yourself.

🧭

Route planner link

Connect Panoptes to your account on byd.serveone.at/ev/: show a code in the car, confirm on your phone, done. Disconnect at any time.

🖥️

EV Cockpit on your PC

The EV Cockpit shows Panoptes data on your PC, including charging sessions and costs, and can change its settings. You confirm the pairing with one tap and a four-digit check number.

⬆️

Updates in the app

If you like, Panoptes looks for new versions itself. Installing only happens while the car is parked with the ignition off. From 0.1.13 Panoptes no longer changes permanent Android settings (such as “screen always on”); values set earlier can be reset.

🌐

Five languages

German, English, French, Italian and Dutch.

How it all fits together

Panoptes runs in the car. Everything else is optional and can be connected and disconnected one by one.

1Panoptes ↔ EV Cockpit (pairing)

You allow the cockpit once with a check number, in Telegram or on the Panoptes page. The cockpit then fetches vehicle data, settings and the trip log straight from the car, over your home network or Tailscale.

What does not flow: remote access, password and ports. None of this goes through our servers; the trip log stays on your PC. The cockpit keeps the access key in your computer's keyring. Anything else it sends to servers (map, 3D model, route planner, usage count) needs its own tick; without a tick, nothing.

2Panoptes ↔ route planner

Once linked, your destination with charging stops goes into the car. Panoptes reports charge level, consumption factor and driving status so the plan is right. If you turn it on (off by default) and fetch your trip log on /ev/, it comes end-to-end encrypted straight from the car to your browser.

What does not flow: the server cannot read the trip log; it only receives encrypted data and, according to the route planner, keeps it in memory for 2 minutes at most. Your approximate position is only sent when Panoptes suggests a new charging stop on the road.

3Panoptes ↔ community map

If you choose it as map source, map tiles and charger data come from byd.serveone.at, with live availability where open data exists. Without a choice Panoptes loads no map.

What does not flow: trips and recordings. The server only sees your IP address and the map area. Shared rules only go to the community server if you share them yourself (off by default).

4Panoptes → Telegram

Your own bot sends you message texts. Pictures and videos only if you explicitly turn that on; they are then stored on Telegram's servers.

What does not flow: no videos on our servers, no detour through the project.

5Panoptes ↔ wallbox (in testing)

Panoptes talks to your wallbox in your home network, via evcc, Home Assistant, Tuya or directly. Control only works while the car is parked and you have enabled the charge point, each change with confirmation. You can withdraw the permission at any time.

What does not flow: none of this goes through our servers. Credentials are stored encrypted in the car. Manufacturer clouds (Easee, Zaptec, Tuya) only with your consent per device.

6Panoptes ↔ Home Assistant (planned)

A direct connection between your car and your own Home Assistant is planned. For the wallbox, Panoptes can already use Home Assistant today.

What does not flow: the data is not meant to be stored with us.

7EV Cockpit ↔ route planner

The cockpit shows the route waiting for you and your charge cards. When it is paired with Panoptes, only Panoptes reports the charge level, so nothing arrives twice.

What does not flow: your trips stay on your PC.

Privacy: what goes where

Principle: your recordings, trips and positions stay in the car. Panoptes does not send camera recordings to the project's servers. Anything that sends data out is off by default; you turn each item on separately, and Panoptes tells you beforehand where the data goes. No ads, no tracking.

What you need

  • A BYD with DiLink 3, 4 or 5 and Android 9 or newer on the car screen.
  • So far Panoptes has mainly been tested on the BYD Dolphin Surf (DiLink 3). On other models some things may be missing or work differently; Panoptes hides what your car cannot do.
  • Storage for camera recordings, ideally a USB stick in the car.
  • A free Tailscale account for remote access and your own Telegram bot for notifications. Panoptes shows you how.

Step by step

  1. Read first. Panoptes is meant for your own car, for private use and at your own risk. Read the notes. To install it you turn on developer functions in the car. If in doubt whether this matters for your warranty, ask your dealer.
  2. Download and check. Download panoptes-0.1.14.1-dfad145.apk to your PC with the button above, then compare the checksum. On Windows type certutil -hashfile panoptes-0.1.14.1-dfad145.apk SHA256 in a command prompt, on a Mac or Linux shasum -a 256 panoptes-0.1.14.1-dfad145.apk. The output must match the checksum above exactly. If it differs, do not install the file.
  3. Turn on developer access (ADB) in the car. Panoptes starts its background services, for example for the cameras, through the car's ADB interface. This connection stays inside the car (127.0.0.1). It is off by default. You turn it on in the BYD developer menu, with the switches for USB debugging or wireless ADB. How to reach this menu depends a lot on model and software version; on newer BYD software it is locked with a code. So far we have only tested this step on the Dolphin Surf (DiLink 3). Without it Panoptes will not fully work.
  4. Get the app onto the car. Tested way, with a PC: install Google's Android platform tools on your PC (they contain adb). PC and car must be on the same network, for example your phone's Wi-Fi hotspot. Then type adb connect <car-address>:5555 and adb install -r panoptes-0.1.14.1-dfad145.apk. You find the car's address in its Wi-Fi settings or in your hotspot's device list. At the end it must say Success. Without a PC (community reports, not tested by us): some BYD software versions install APK files straight from a USB stick through the built-in installer. Folder name and procedure differ by region and software. If Android asks about “installing from unknown sources”, allow it only for this installation.
  5. First start with the setup assistant. Open Panoptes on the car screen. The assistant guides you step by step through everything needed: language, confirming the notes, autostart, display over other apps, permissions, USB access, your car and cameras, guard, operating mode, map source, data sharing, speed cameras and updates. Then optional extras: Telegram, route planner, EV Cockpit and remote access. Nothing is preselected. Anything that sends data out stays off until you turn it on yourself.
  6. Allow autostart. For Panoptes to start by itself when you get in, it has to be allowed in BYD's app management. The setup assistant opens the right page; untick the autostart block for Panoptes there. BYD resets this switch after every installation and update. Panoptes will remind you. DiLink 5 has no such app management, so the step is skipped.
  7. Confirm USB access. Android asks once “Allow USB debugging?”. Tick “Always allow from this computer” and tap Allow. The “computer” is Panoptes itself in the car. Without the tick Android asks again after every restart.
  8. Open on your phone or PC (optional). With remote access (Tailscale) set up, open your car's address in a browser. To log in you need the access code, found in the app in the car under Home → Access code. You allow other devices, such as the EV Cockpit, under System → Pair a device.

If something goes wrong

  • Panoptes does not start by itself after an update: allow autostart again (step 6). BYD resets it after every installation.
  • Panoptes has no internet: BYD's built-in traffic monitor can block the internet for other apps. Panoptes offers to switch it off; the screen then needs a restart.
  • Cameras or guard do not run: check that USB access was confirmed with “Always allow” (step 7) and that ADB access is still on; a BYD software update can reset it.
  • Where to store recordings? Ideally on a USB stick in the car.
  • Removing it again: uninstall in the car's Android settings under Apps. Data inside the app is lost; recordings on the USB stick stay.
